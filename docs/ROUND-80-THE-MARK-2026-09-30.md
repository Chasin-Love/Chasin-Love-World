# ROUND 80 — THE MARK (2026-09-30)

> The placeholder dies. The app finally wears its own cosmos on its face:
> the icon, the taskbar, the browser tab. And when the author's own sigil
> arrived — a blue four-pointed star burning out of a dark nebula — it took
> the throne, and the analytic mark it briefly served became the fallback
> (and a keepsake on the author's desktop).

## What happened

The author's complaint: the app icon was "too nasty to look at." True — it was
the foundation-era placeholder (a teal/violet disc generated by
`scripts/make-icons.mjs` "so the build is unblocked"). It was never artwork.

## The sigil (the official face)

The author supplied `logo.png` (a 736×736 JPEG of a blue four-pointed star in
a swirl nebula) and its verdict: "sharp the photo & specially the pixels &
make the photo as logo." `scripts/make-icons.mjs` now renders it through a
photo pipeline in the project's own Playwright Chromium:

- **center-crop to fill** the square (crop center 0.50/0.48),
- **unsharp mask** (3×3 box blur, per-size amount — heavy at 512, nearly none
  at 32 where halos read as static),
- **S-curve contrast** + **levels** (gamma lift raises the nebula out of
  black; the white point pulls the star's core to burning white),
- **per-size crop zoom** — 1.22× at 512, 2.30× at 32 — because the outer
  swirls are noise at taskbar size and the core star IS the logo there.

Each size renders directly from the source photo (no halving-chain softness).
The master photo lives at `src-tauri/icons/logo-master.jpg`.

## The mark (the fallback face)

`node scripts/make-icons.mjs --eventide` renders the analytic **Eventide at
rest** — a black event-horizon pit wearing its accretion ring (white-hot teal
edge → teal-ice → gold fringe), wrapped in the violet lens halo, tilted
toward the viewer, on the abyss square (#010208, corners kept). Pure
per-pixel analytic rendering, zero image deps. Craft notes kept from its
two failed drafts: v1 blew out to white (a units bug — 0–255 values run
through a 0–1 clamp), and v2 washed pastel because the teal→solar blend
spent too long in the milky mid-ramp; v3 compresses the ramp and drops the
streak frequency to 9 so supersampling doesn't moiré. Both faces of the icon
are saved as PNGs on the author's desktop.

## The RC2175 lesson (the build that had to fail)

The first v15.0.5 tag built green on Linux and FAILED on Windows at the NSIS
step: `RC2175`, the resource compiler rejecting the icon. Root cause: the
old generator wrapped a PNG inside the ICO *and* left the type field zero —
labeling it a cursor. Windows RC requires classic 32bpp BITMAPINFOHEADER DIB
entries. The repo had hit this once before by hand (commit `a7a299c5`, which
replaced the file with a proper DIB) — the script quietly kept the landmine.
Now the generator writes DIBs itself and is immune. Both failures are
pinned in code comments.

## Release ritual (learned this session, written down)

Releases do not happen on the laptop and never needed to. The pipeline
(`.github/workflows/desktop.yml`) builds on GitHub's Windows runner (MSVC
present) on every `v*` tag push, generates `latest.json`, and publishes the
release. The previous session's "cannot build" was a local-toolchain dead end
that never mattered. The v15.0.4 intro-loop fix proved the ritual; v15.0.5
repeats it — and its first failed run proved something more: the pipeline is
also the safety net that catches what the laptop cannot see (RC2175 lived
only on Windows). A failed tag is fixed and the tag is re-pushed; the release
only ever publishes green.

Housekeeping fixed in passing: `Cargo.toml` had drifted to 15.0.2 while
`tauri.conf.json` rode at 15.0.4 — both now move in lockstep at 15.0.5.
