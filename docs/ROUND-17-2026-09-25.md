## The identification that drove the round

The user's correction (with diagrams): a black hole is **not an object floating on the
universe** — it is a **hole in the surface of the universe itself**, shaped like an
**ice-cream cone** placed mouth-down in water: the mouth circle sits on the surface,
the cone plunges beneath, and the water bends inward around it and pours down the
sides. Round 16 bent the *light* of the sky; the *surface itself* never dipped. That
was the "two layers" complaint — a bent sky underneath, an unbent photo on top, and
no geometric connection between hole and surface.

## What was built

### 1. The funnel — the exact 1916 embedding of Schwarzschild spacetime

`blackhole.ts` — `createSpacetimeFunnel(R, colorA, colorB)`:

- **Flamm's paraboloid** (Flamm 1916), the exact spatial embedding of the
  Schwarzschild metric: **z(r) = 2·√(rs·(r − rs))** — the classic "grid sinking
  into a funnel" diagram of general relativity, drawn by physics, not art.
  Exported as `flammDepth(rRs)` so the gauntlet can verify the shader's own
  geometry function.
- One revolution surface: 44 **log-spaced** rings × 72 segments, uniform in log r —
  the grid densifies toward the throat exactly like the textbook figure, and a
  uniform inward drift of ring phase reads as accelerating infall.
- The throat emerges at r = 1 rs **inside the composite's own black horizon
  sphere** (which occludes it) — funnel and shadow are **one object**: the
  surface pouring into the hole, no seam, no sticker.
- Grid lines (32 spokes + flowing circles) drawn in-shader with derivative
  anti-aliasing (`fwidth`), tinted by the **active reality's palette** and
  slowly pouring inward — the waterfall of the analogy.
- Outer melt 16→30 rs: the funnel has no edge, only curvature dissolving into
  the (already Schwarzschild-bent) sky. **Lens · Clear** flattens it away —
  its strength rides the damped `lensCur` toggle. Pure visuals: no forces, no
  orbit changes, Dimensional Anchor and Living Gravity untouched.

### 2. The photo-sky bug — found, fixed, proven

`photoDome.ts` had **zero lens uniforms**: an uploaded photo background sat
unbent above the bent procedural sky — the detached "two layers" look.

- PhotoDome now receives UniverseSurfaceManager's **one shared lens uniform
  object set** (by reference, zero per-frame cost) and runs the full Round-16
  Schwarzschild law in **both** stages: per-vertex for coarse bending, per-pixel

- The bent direction drives the photo's UVs through the **exact inverse of

  **no** +0.5 offset) — a hand-rolled convention here would rotate and mirror
  every photo sky. Inside b_c the photo is painted **fully transparent** — a
  true hole in the picture, matching the procedural dome's shadow.

## Round 17.1 — the user's two laws (same day, R42)

The user reviewed R41 and issued two absolute corrections — both accepted as
laws and enforced in code *and* tests:

1. **The cone plunges along −Z, the view axis.** The viewer must never see
   the cone's geometry — only the void: black, emptiness, nothingness. → The
   funnel membrane was rebuilt: rings in the local XY plane, Flamm depth
   sinking along −Z, and the funnel group **billboarded to the camera** every
   frame — the mouth always faces the viewer dead-on, circles sinking into
   the shadow's black.
2. **The pour is always circular.** However the surface distorts, the mouth
   touches every tangential point equally — concentric rings only, never
   spokes, never polygons. → The 32 angular spokes were **deleted** from the
   shader; the pattern is now a pure function of radius (`r` only — the
   gauntlet *greps the shader source* to prove no `theta`/`atan`/`cos`/`sin`
   exists). Additionally the **photo-dome double-bend was killed**: the
   vertex stage had bent per coarse vertex and the fragment bent again — the
   interpolated double application was the angular facet artifact in the
   user's screenshot. The vertex stage now passes the raw direction; the
   per-pixel fragment bend is the single source of truth.

**Verification:** gauntlet now 14/14 (two law checks added), typecheck +
production build green, **BUILD R42** live with a clean console.

## Round 18.1 — why the user saw nothing change (R45)

The user's screenshot showed the hole against their **bright photo sky** —
and every glow layer of the disk and halo used **additive blending**. Gold
light added onto a white background is white: the new disk and halo were
rendering the whole time, invisibly, washed out by the sky. All reference
stills show the hole against dark space.

**The fix:** the disk, the lensed halo, the photon ring and the haze now use
**normal blending** — they are dense matter that *occludes* the sky behind
them, exactly like the references. They now read over any background, bright
or dark. A permanent gauntlet check (17 total) enforces the blending so it
can never regress.

## Round 18.2 — the stuck background on reality switch (R46)

The user's report: after switching realities, the previous reality's photo
background lingered — sometimes forever, needing a hard refresh. The
reality switch itself was clean; the **PhotoDome crossfade** was the culprit:

- The crossfade deliberately **holds** while a new photo loads (so the old
  sky doesn't flash away mid-load) — but that hold had **no way out** when
  the new photo was slow, failed, or *absent* (reality with no sky): the
  old photo stayed at ~90% opacity indefinitely.

**The fix:** an explicit pending-load state (`pendingLoad`/`pendingSince`)
— the hold releases the moment the load lands *or fails*, and a 4-second
backstop expires it no matter what. Verified in the live app with the real
PhotoDome across four scenarios: photo enters ✓, switch to a photo reality
whose image FAILS → old sky releases ✓, switch to a no-photo reality →
releases immediately ✓, photo returns → sky re-enters ✓. Reality isolation
of backgrounds is now exact: each reality's sky belongs to that reality
alone, and leaving it always lets go.

## Round 18.3 — continuity: the disk is ONE structure (R47)

The user compared R45's hole to the stills and named the remaining flaw
precisely: in the references the disk is **one continuous, smooth,
glorifying thing** — the band may narrow and dim, but it never stops
existing. In ours, whole arcs of the circular wrap nearly vanished at the
sides, and the horizontal blade had sectors that faded to nothing.

Three causes, three fixes:
1. **Filament noise crushed alpha to zero** in its dark pockets → floors
   raised (streak modulates brightness 0.55–1.0, never existence; halo glow
   floor likewise).
2. **The Doppler dim-side multiplied those holes darker** → dim-side floor
   0.40 → 0.52 (the references' dim side is still plainly luminous).
3. **The halo mesh sampled outside its texture's painted band** — the wrap
   began at texture r=0.5 but the mesh's inner radius mapped to ~0.42,
   leaving a structural gap at the shadow's limb → the wrap now paints from
   r=0.425, hugging the limb, with an explicit body floor (α ≥ 0.30 within
   the band) so no arc can ever vanish.

**Proven with 720 fresh pixel samples in the live app**: disk 0/360 dead
sectors (min 81 / max 146, contrast 1.8×), halo 0/360 dead at mid-band AND
at the limb, white-hot edge intact. The blade breathes — it never breaks.
